Find the cause, then repair the layout

The production pages had no horizontal overflow in the initial checks. This isolated exercise compares a broken card with its repaired version inside the same narrow inspection region.

The broken example has a local horizontal scrollbar so its entire box can be inspected. The page itself stays within the viewport; no content is hidden.

1. Broken: fixed width plus padding

Focus the inspection region below and use the arrow keys to scroll sideways. Inspect the card and its parent using browser developer tools.

Web Development

A fixed-width card does not adapt to the available space.

Its content width is 32rem. With content-box sizing, padding and borders are added to that width, making the rendered box even wider.

  • Root cause: width: 32rem exceeds the parent; box-sizing: content-box adds padding and borders outside it.
  • Diagnosis: the region's scrollWidth is larger than its clientWidth. The card's box model shows the extra width.

2. Repaired: use the available width

Web Development

A flexible card adapts to the available space.

Its width is 100% of the parent. Border-box sizing includes its padding and borders within that width, and text wraps naturally.

  • Repair: use width: 100%, max-width: 100%, min-width: 0, and box-sizing: border-box.
  • Result: the region's scrollWidth equals its clientWidth. The entire card is visible without sideways scrolling.

Try the repair yourself

  1. Inspect at viewport widths of 320, 390, 768, and 1366 pixels.
  2. Compare the broken card's content, padding, border, and total width.
  3. Change its width to 100% and its box sizing to border-box in developer tools.
  4. Confirm the local scrollbar disappears and explain why.
  5. Inspect the production card grid: its minimum track width is capped at 100% so even narrow containers can hold a card.
