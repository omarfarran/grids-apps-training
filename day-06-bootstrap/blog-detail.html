Planning

Start with a brief your team can use.

By Omar Farran · 22 October 2026 · 4 minute read

A useful brief gives a team a common starting point. It explains the problem, the people affected, and the result worth working toward.

Describe the problem before the features

Instead of starting with “we need a dashboard,” describe the difficulty: project owners cannot see which milestones need attention without asking several people. That statement helps the team judge whether a proposed feature solves the actual problem.

Agree what a useful first release includes

Choose a small outcome you can review. A first version might show current projects, their owners, and the next milestone. Record what is outside the scope so new ideas can be considered without silently changing the plan.

A brief in five questions

  1. Who needs help, and what are they trying to do?
  2. What makes the current process difficult?
  3. What would a useful improvement look like?
  4. Which constraints and dependencies matter?
  5. Who reviews the result, and how will they decide it is ready?

Keep the brief alive

Return to the brief at each review. Record decisions and explain changes to the scope. A short document that stays current is easier to use than an ambitious plan no one trusts.

Make feedback easier to act on

Start each review with a specific question. Ask whether the milestone view makes priorities clear, rather than asking for general thoughts about the whole interface.

Keep notes together and distinguish an observation from a requested change. Assign an owner to each decision and confirm what will happen before the next review. When feedback conflicts, return to the goal in the brief.

A progress report that earns its place

A report should help someone choose a next step. Lead with the outcome of the week, identify anything blocking delivery, and name the next milestone and its owner.

Use numbers with context. A completion percentage is useful only when readers understand what is included and what remains. Pair it with a short explanation of the next review or unresolved risk.
